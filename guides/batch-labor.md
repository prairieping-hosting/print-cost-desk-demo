# Separating batch setup from per-item labor

A batch quote is easier to explain when shared setup work and per-item labor are recorded separately. Setup can include preparing files, arranging the workspace and getting the job ready to run. Per-item labor can include removing supports, finishing, inspecting and packing each piece.

Start by estimating the setup time for the whole batch. Then estimate the hands-on time needed for each item and multiply that by the batch quantity. Add those times together and apply your chosen hourly labor rate. To estimate labor per item, divide the total batch labor cost by the quantity.

Charge shared setup once for the batch. If an item needs its own preparation or a customer requests a change that creates additional setup work, record that work separately so the quote remains understandable.

Keep notes on what your labor estimate includes. After completing a job, compare estimated time with actual hands-on time and use the difference to improve future quotes. Machine runtime and hands-on labor should be recorded separately; a machine running unattended does not automatically mean continuous labor.

This calculation covers labor only. Add material, machine time, packaging, selling fees, applicable taxes and other overhead where relevant, then account for your intended margin. Estimates depend on your workflow and chosen labor rate; they do not establish a market price.

## Worked reference

Synthetic example: a batch takes 15 minutes setup plus 3 minutes per item for 5 items: total labor 30 minutes.

At an hourly labor rate of 20.00, batch labor is 10.00, or 2.00 per item.

Do not charge the full setup time again for each item in the same batch.

Material, machine time, fees and margin must be added separately.
